What Is the EU Blue Card?

The EU Blue Card is an EU‑wide work‑and‑residence permit for highly qualified non‑EU nationals. It was created to attract skilled professionals to Europe and is active in 25 of the 27 EU Member States — Denmark and Ireland do not participate. The program was updated under the revised Blue Card Directive (EU) 2021/1883, which EU countries implemented by November 2023.

Unlike standard work permits, the EU Blue Card offers:

  • Mobility — work and live in any participating EU country.
  • Fast‑track permanent residency — after 33 months (or 21 months with B1 language skills).
  • Family reunification — bring your spouse and children with simplified procedures.
  • Intra‑EU mobility — move to a second Member State after 12 months without repeating a labour market test.

EU Blue Card Salary Thresholds 2026

To qualify for an EU Blue Card, you must have a job offer with a salary that meets or exceeds the threshold set by the country you are applying to. The EU directive sets a floor, but each Member State can set its own higher threshold.

Germany (2026)

  • General threshold: €50,700 gross per year
  • Shortage occupations (STEM, IT, healthcare): €45,934.20
  • Applicants over 45: €55,770

Other Key Countries (2026)

  • France: €59,373
  • Spain: €41,000
  • Luxembourg: €65,652
  • Belgium: €68,815 for senior, €4,748/month for junior
  • Netherlands: €5,942 (reduced threshold)

Note: Thresholds are updated annually and may vary by country. Always check the official immigration website of your target country.

EU Blue Card Eligibility Requirements

  • Non‑EU nationality — you must be a citizen of a country outside the EU.
  • Higher education degree — a university degree or equivalent qualification (at least 3 years of study).
  • Job offer — a valid employment contract or binding job offer from an employer in the EU country.
  • Salary threshold — the job must meet the minimum salary requirement for that country.
  • Work experience — in some cases, professional experience can substitute for a degree.

Nationals of countries such as the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia can enter Germany without a visa and apply for the Blue Card after arrival.

How to Apply for an EU Blue Card

  1. Secure a job offer — find an employer in an EU country who is willing to sponsor you.
  2. Check eligibility — ensure your degree and salary meet the requirements.
  3. Submit a visa application — apply at the embassy or consulate of the target country in your home country.
  4. Gather required documents — passport, degree certificates, employment contract, biometric photos, and application forms.
  5. Pay the fee — typically around €100–€140.
  6. Wait for processing — processing times vary by country.
  7. Enter the country — once approved, you can travel and begin work.

In some countries, applications can be submitted online. For example, in Poland, applications must be submitted through the Case Handling Module (MOS) portal.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the EU Blue Card?
The EU Blue Card is a work‑and‑residence permit for highly qualified non‑EU nationals, valid in 25 EU countries. It offers a streamlined path to permanent residency and family reunification.
What is the EU Blue Card salary threshold for 2026?
The general threshold is €50,700. For shortage occupations and recent graduates, the reduced threshold is €45,934.20. These vary by country — France requires €59,373, while Spain requires €41,000.
Which countries issue the EU Blue Card?
25 EU Member States issue the Blue Card. Denmark and Ireland do not participate. The top issuers include Germany, the Netherlands, France, Sweden, and Spain.
How long does it take to get permanent residency with an EU Blue Card?
You can apply after 33 months (or 21 months with B1 language proficiency). After 5 years of legal residence in the EU, you can apply for EU long‑term resident status.
Can I bring my family with an EU Blue Card?
Yes. EU Blue Card holders have facilitated family reunification rights. Spouses and dependent children can join and are usually granted work permits.
Do I need a job offer before applying?
Yes. A valid employment contract or binding job offer from an employer in the EU country is mandatory for the EU Blue Card.
