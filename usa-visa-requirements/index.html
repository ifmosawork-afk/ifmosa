📋 General U.S. Visa Eligibility & Required Documents

While each visa category has specific requirements, all U.S. work visa applicants must generally meet these criteria and provide the following documents:

  • Valid passport – Must be valid for at least 6 months beyond your intended stay in the U.S.
  • Job offer from a U.S. employer – For most work visas (H1B, H2B, L1, O1, TN, E3). Some non‑work visas (B1/B2) do not require a job offer.
  • Employer petition approval (I‑797) – For H1B, H2B, L1, O1, TN, and E3, the employer must file a petition (Form I‑129) with USCIS and receive approval before you apply for the visa.
  • Proof of qualifications – Diplomas, transcripts, professional licenses, or detailed work experience letters demonstrating you meet the job requirements.
  • Non‑immigrant intent (for most work visas) – You must show you have a residence abroad and intend to return after your authorized stay, unless the visa allows dual intent (e.g., H1B, L1).
  • Visa application fee receipt – Pay the MRV fee ($205 for most work visas).
  • Photo – One recent 2x2 inch photograph meeting State Department requirements.
  • Supporting evidence for the specific visa category – See sections below.

📌 Detailed Requirements by Visa Category

💼 H1B Visa Requirements

  • Job must be a "specialty occupation" – typically requires a bachelor's degree or higher in a specific field.
  • Employer must file a Labor Condition Application (LCA) with DOL and pay the required wages.
  • Subject to annual cap of 65,000 (plus 20,000 for U.S. master's degree holders) unless cap‑exempt (universities/non‑profits).
  • Visa validity: Up to 3 years, renewable to 6 years total.

🌾 H2B Visa Requirements

  • Job must be temporary, seasonal, or a peak‑load need (non‑agricultural).
  • Employer must obtain a temporary labor certification from DOL proving no qualified U.S. workers are available.
  • Annual cap: 66,000 visas per fiscal year (33,000 per half‑year). Supplemental caps may be available.
  • Visa validity: Up to 1 year, renewable in 1‑year increments for a maximum of 3 years total.

🏢 L1 Visa Requirements

  • Must have been employed abroad by a parent, branch, affiliate, or subsidiary of the U.S. employer for at least 1 continuous year within the last 3 years.
  • L1A (managerial/executive) – renewable up to 7 years total.
  • L1B (specialized knowledge) – renewable up to 5 years total.
  • No annual cap; allows dual intent (can apply for green card immediately).

🏅 O1 Visa Requirements

  • Extraordinary ability evidenced by sustained national or international acclaim (e.g., major awards, publications, judging, high salary).
  • Requires a written consultation from a peer group or labor organization.
  • No annual cap; allows dual intent.
  • Visa validity: Initial 3 years, renewable in 1‑year increments.

🇨🇦🇲🇽 TN Visa (USMCA – Canadian & Mexican Citizens)

  • Only for citizens of Canada or Mexico (permanent residents not eligible).
  • Job must be on the list of 60+ professional occupations (e.g., engineer, accountant, scientist, computer systems analyst).
  • Bachelor's degree or relevant licensure required for most occupations.
  • No USCIS petition required for Canadians (apply at border/airport with job offer). Mexicans need a visa stamp at a consulate.
  • Visa validity: 3 years, renewable indefinitely.

🇦🇺 E3 Visa (Australian Citizens Only)

  • Similar to H1B but exclusively for Australian citizens.
  • Specialty occupation requiring a bachelor's degree.
  • Employer must file a Labor Condition Application (LCA).
  • No annual cap (10,500 visas available, never reached).
  • Spouse (any nationality) receives work authorization automatically.
  • Visa validity: 2 years, renewable indefinitely.

✈️ B1/B2 Visa (Business/Tourism – Not for Employment)

  • For short‑term business activities (attending conferences, negotiating contracts, consulting with business associates).
  • Cannot be employed by a U.S. entity or receive salary from a U.S. source.
  • Typical stay: 6 months (extensions possible).
  • Applicants must show strong ties to home country and non‑immigrant intent.

📝 Step‑by‑Step U.S. Work Visa Application Process

  1. Find a U.S. employer willing to sponsor you – Use IFMOSA Work's job board to find verified sponsors.
  2. Employer files a petition (if required) – For H1B, H2B, L1, O1, E3, and TN (for Mexicans), the employer files Form I‑129 with USCIS.
  3. USCIS approves the petition (I‑797) – Processing times vary; premium processing available for most categories.
  4. Pay the visa application fee ($205 for most work visas) – Fee varies by category.
  5. Complete Form DS‑160 (Online Nonimmigrant Visa Application) – Submit online and print the confirmation page.
  6. Schedule visa interview at a U.S. embassy or consulate – Wait times vary by location and time of year.
  7. Attend the interview – Bring your passport, DS‑160 confirmation, I‑797 approval notice (if applicable), job offer letter, proof of qualifications, and evidence of ties to home country (if required).
  8. Visa approval & passport collection – If approved, the visa stamp is placed in your passport.
  9. Travel to the U.S. and enter on or after the start date – For H1B, the earliest entry date is October 1 (if subject to cap).

⏱️ Total timeline: 3–9 months depending on visa category, USCIS processing, and embassy appointment availability.

💡 U.S. Visa Interview Tips – How to Succeed

  • Be honest and concise – Answer questions directly without volunteering unnecessary information.
  • Demonstrate non‑immigrant intent (if required) – Explain your ties to your home country (family, property, job prospects). For dual‑intent visas (H1B, L1, O1), this is not required.
  • Know your job and employer – Be prepared to describe your duties, company, and why you were hired.
  • Bring all required documents – Use the “Documents Checklist” below.
  • Dress professionally – First impressions matter.
  • Stay calm and confident – The officer is not trying to trick you; they just need to verify your eligibility.

  • ✅ Valid passport (with at least 6 months validity beyond intended stay)
  • ✅ DS‑160 confirmation page (with barcode)

  • ✅ Resume/CV (tailored to the job)
  • ✅ Work experience letters (if applicable)
  • ✅ Proof of ties to home country (property deeds, family certificates, bank statements – for non‑dual intent visas)
  • ✅ Previous U.S. visas (if any)

⚠️ Common U.S. Visa Application Mistakes & Scams

  • Filing the wrong visa category – Applying for H1B when you qualify for L1 can waste time and money. Always consult a qualified immigration attorney.
  • Missing the annual cap deadlines (H1B, H2B) – For H1B, registration opens in March; for H2B, petitions are accepted months in advance of the start date. Plan early.
  • Submitting false documents – Using fake degrees or work experience letters leads to permanent inadmissibility and a ban.
  • Paying an “agent” to guarantee a visa – No one can guarantee a visa. Only the U.S. embassy or USCIS can approve a visa.
  • Employer asking you to pay filing fees – For H1B, H2B, L1, O1, and EB‑3, the employer must pay the I‑129 and related fees. If they ask you to pay, it's illegal.
  • Not maintaining status while I‑485 is pending – If you file adjustment of status, you must maintain lawful status until your I‑485 has been pending for 180 days.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions – USA Visa Requirements

Can I work in the U.S. without a work visa?
No. Working in the U.S. without proper authorization is illegal and can result in deportation and a ban from re‑entry.
What is the difference between a visa and a work permit?
A visa allows you to enter the U.S. for a specific purpose (e.g., work). A work permit (EAD) allows you to work for any employer. Most work visas (H1B, L1, O1) already include work authorization tied to the sponsoring employer.
Can I change employers while on a work visa?
For H1B, you can transfer to a new employer as long as the new employer files a new I‑129 petition. L1 and O1 generally cannot be transferred; you need a new petition from the new employer. H2B does not allow employer transfers.
What is visa “premium processing”?
Premium processing is an optional service that guarantees USCIS action within 15 calendar days for an additional fee ($2,965 for most employment petitions as of March 1, 2026). Available for H1B, L1, O1, and other categories.
How long does a visa interview take?
The interview itself is usually 3–5 minutes. However, waiting times for appointments can range from a few days to several months, depending on the embassy location and time of year.
Can I apply for a U.S. work visa without a job offer?
For most employment‑based visas (H1B, H2B, L1, O1, E3, TN), you must have a confirmed job offer from a U.S. employer. The only exceptions are self‑petition categories like EB‑1 (extraordinary ability) or EB‑2 NIW, which are green card categories, not temporary work visas.
