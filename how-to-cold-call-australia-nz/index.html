Why Cold Calling Works in Australia & New Zealand

Australia and New Zealand share some cultural similarities, but their hiring cultures, communication styles, and visa systems have distinct differences. Cold calling allows you to:

  • Bypass online application systems and speak directly to decision‑makers.
  • Discover unadvertised roles – many ANZ positions are filled through networking and direct approaches.
  • Demonstrate the proactive mindset that ANZ employers appreciate.

Key insight: In both countries, a casual but professional tone works well. However, Australians tend to be more direct and fast‑paced, while New Zealanders are more relationship‑focused and informal. Understanding these nuances is critical to success.

Visa context – Australia: The main employer‑sponsored visa is the Subclass 482 (Skills in Demand) visa. From 1 July 2026, the Core Skills Income Threshold (CSIT) increased to AUD $79,499 per year (up from $76,515)[reference:0][reference:1]. The Specialist Skills Income Threshold (SSIT) is now AUD $146,717[reference:2]. Employers must meet these salary requirements and pass Labour Market Testing (LMT) for most nominations[reference:3].

Visa context – New Zealand: The main employer‑sponsored visa is the Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV). As of 2026, there are over 24,800 accredited employers in New Zealand[reference:4]. From 1 June 2026, English language requirements were expanded to include ANZSCO Skill Level 3 occupations, requiring a minimum IELTS score of 4.0 or equivalent[reference:5][reference:6]. The AEWV requires a job offer of at least 30 hours per week[reference:7].

🇦🇺 Australia – Culture, Visas & Cold Calling Scripts

🇦🇺 Australia

Cultural Characteristics

  • Direct and fast‑paced – Australians value efficiency and straight talk. Cold calling is common in sales and business development roles[reference:8].
  • Casual but professional – first names are used quickly, but respect for seniority remains. A friendly "How are you?" is expected before getting to business[reference:9].
  • Proactive approach is valued – contacting the head office directly to express interest is not only acceptable but can be highly advantageous[reference:10].
  • Projects create employment – following infrastructure, mining, renewable energy, and construction projects can reveal unadvertised roles[reference:11].
  • Right to disconnect – Australia has a right‑to‑disconnect law; avoid calling outside of standard business hours[reference:12].

Visa Context – Subclass 482 (Skills in Demand)

  • Salary thresholds (from 1 July 2026): Core Skills Income Threshold (CSIT) – AUD $79,499 per year[reference:13]. Specialist Skills Income Threshold (SSIT) – AUD $146,717 per year[reference:14].
  • Employer requirements: Must be an approved sponsor, meet compliance obligations, and pass Labour Market Testing (LMT) for most nominations[reference:15][reference:16].
  • Pathway to PR: The Subclass 186 (Employer Nomination Scheme) visa offers a direct pathway to permanent residency after meeting certain requirements.

Best Time to Call

  • Days: Tuesday – Thursday.
  • Time (AEST/AEDT): 10:00 – 11:30 AM or 2:00 – 4:00 PM. Avoid Monday mornings and Friday afternoons.
  • Time zones: Australia has three time zones – be mindful when calling Western Australia (AWST) from the east coast.

Scripts – Australia (direct, confident, results‑focused)

Scenario A: You found a job ad

"G'day [Hiring Manager's Name], this is [Your Full Name]. I saw your ad for the [Role] position on [Platform] and I'm really keen. I've got [X] years of experience in [field] – I've worked on [specific project or achievement] – and I think I could hit the ground running. I don't want to take up too much of your time, but I'd love to send you my CV directly and have a quick chat about what you're looking for. Would that be alright?"

Note: Australians appreciate a direct, no‑nonsense approach. A friendly "G'day" is acceptable in most industries. Lead with your value and be concise.

Scenario B: Exploring unadvertised roles (project‑based approach)

"Hello [Hiring Manager's Name], this is [Your Full Name]. I've been following [Company] and I see you're involved in [specific project or industry]. I've got experience in [skill] and I've delivered [quantifiable result, e.g., 20% cost reduction, 50+ projects]. I'm not sure if you have anything open right now, but I wanted to introduce myself and ask if you'd be open to a brief chat about your team's priorities. I'm happy to send my CV through."

Note: Australian employers respond well to candidates who understand their industry and projects. Mentioning specific projects shows you've done your homework[reference:17].

Scenario C: You need TSS 482 sponsorship

"Good morning [Hiring Manager's Name], this is [Your Full Name]. I'm really interested in [Company] and I believe my experience in [field] could be a strong fit. I want to be upfront – I'm currently based outside Australia and I'd need sponsorship under the 482 visa. I know the salary threshold is around $79,500 now[reference:18], and I'm confident my skills justify that. Is that something your company is open to for the right candidate?"

Note: Showing you understand the visa requirements (CSIT threshold) demonstrates preparation and professionalism. It also saves time by confirming sponsorship feasibility early[reference:19].

Scenario D: Following up after an application

"Hi [Hiring Manager's Name], this is [Your Full Name]. I submitted an application for the [Role] position a few days ago and I just wanted to follow up. I'm really keen on the opportunity – I've got [X] years in [field] and I think I could add real value to your team. I'm not expecting a decision right now, I just wanted to put a voice to the application. Would you have a couple of minutes?"

Follow‑up email (professional and concise)

Subject: Follow‑up – [Your Full Name] – [Role] interest

G'day [Hiring Manager's Name],

Thanks for taking the time to speak with me earlier. I really appreciated your insights into the [Role] and what your team is working on.

As I mentioned, I've got [X] years of experience in [field] and I'm confident I could contribute to [Company]'s goals. I've attached my CV for your reference – let me know if you need anything else.

Cheers,
[Your Full Name]

Common Objections – Australia

"We don't sponsor 482 visas."

Response: "No worries – is that a fixed policy, or could it vary by role? I'm open to exploring other options if that's possible. Thanks for your time."

"We prefer local candidates."

Response: "Fair enough. If my skills are a strong match, would you consider sponsorship? I'm confident I can add real value to your team."

"We don't have a sponsor licence."

Response: "I understand. Is that something the company is considering? I appreciate you being upfront."

Australia‑Specific FAQ

Is cold calling common in Australia?
Yes – it's widely used in sales, business development, and increasingly in job searching. Around 10% of cold calls turn into positive conversations, and about 5% progress to meetings[reference:20].
Should I call or email first?
Both can work. Many job seekers find success with a direct call, while others prefer a short email followed by a call[reference:21]. A hybrid approach – email then call – is often most effective.
What is the right to disconnect?
Australia has a right‑to‑disconnect law that means you shouldn't call outside of standard business hours (9‑5) unless agreed[reference:22].

🇳🇿 New Zealand – Culture, Visas & Cold Calling Scripts

🇳🇿 New Zealand

Cultural Characteristics

  • Relationship‑focused and informal – Kiwis value personal connections and a friendly approach. A "How are you?" is expected before getting to business[reference:23].
  • Casual but respectful – first names are used quickly, but respect for seniority and experience remains.
  • Hidden job market – many jobs in New Zealand are never advertised, making cold calling and networking essential[reference:24].
  • Friendly persistence – Kiwis appreciate genuine interest and follow‑up, but avoid being pushy. Cold calling is common in sales and recruitment roles[reference:25].
  • Humans‑first approach – candidates prefer a "humans‑first" experience over cold, algorithmic processes[reference:26].

Visa Context – Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV)

  • Accredited employers: As of 2026, there are over 24,800 accredited employers in New Zealand[reference:27]. Accreditation lasts for 12‑24 months[reference:28].
  • English language requirements (from 1 June 2026): Minimum IELTS 4.0 or equivalent for ANZSCO Skill Level 3 occupations[reference:29].
  • Job offer requirements: Minimum 30 hours per week, and the employer must pass the job check stage[reference:30][reference:31].
  • Salary threshold: From August 2026, AEWV applicants must have an annual salary of NZD $123,385.60 per year, or the occupation must be on the Green List to be exempt from Labour Market Testing[reference:32].

Best Time to Call

  • Days: Tuesday – Thursday. Avoid Monday mornings and Friday afternoons[reference:33].
  • Time (NZST/NZDT): 10:00 – 11:30 AM or 2:00 – 4:00 PM. Avoid lunch (12‑1 PM).
  • Note: New Zealand is 2 hours ahead of Australian Eastern Standard Time – be mindful when calling from Australia.

Scripts – New Zealand (friendly, relationship‑focused, genuine)

Scenario A: You found a job ad

"Kia ora [Hiring Manager's Name], this is [Your Full Name]. I hope you're having a good day. I saw your ad for the [Role] position on [Platform] and I'm really interested. I've got [X] years of experience in [field] and I've worked on [specific project]. I don't want to take up too much of your time, but I'd love to send you my CV and have a quick chat about the role. Would that be okay?"

Note: "Kia ora" is a Māori greeting widely used in New Zealand – it shows cultural awareness and respect. Kiwis appreciate a friendly, genuine approach.

Scenario B: Exploring unadvertised roles

"Hello [Hiring Manager's Name], this is [Your Full Name]. I've been following [Company] and I'm really impressed by your work in [area]. I've got experience in [skill] and I'm currently exploring opportunities where I can apply that experience. I understand you might not have anything open right now, but I wanted to introduce myself and ask if you'd be open to a brief conversation about your team's needs. I'm happy to send my CV through."

Note: In New Zealand, many jobs are never advertised[reference:34]. A proactive, friendly approach can uncover hidden opportunities.

Scenario C: You need AEWV sponsorship

"Good morning [Hiring Manager's Name], this is [Your Full Name]. I'm really interested in [Company] and I believe my experience in [field] could be a good fit. I want to be upfront – I'm currently based outside New Zealand and I'd need sponsorship under the AEWV. I understand your company needs to be accredited – I've checked and I see you are[reference:35]. I'm confident I can meet the English requirements[reference:36] and I'm keen to have a conversation. Is that something you'd consider?"

Note: Showing you've checked their accreditation status and understand the English requirements demonstrates preparation and professionalism.

Scenario D: Following up after an application

"Kia ora [Hiring Manager's Name], this is [Your Full Name]. I submitted an application for the [Role] position a few days ago and I just wanted to follow up. I'm really keen on the opportunity – I've got [X] years in [field] and I think I could add real value. Would you have a few minutes to chat?"

Follow‑up email (friendly and professional)

Subject: Follow‑up – [Your Full Name] – [Role] interest

Kia ora [Hiring Manager's Name],

Thanks for taking the time to speak with me earlier. I really appreciated your insights into the [Role] and what your team is working on.

As I mentioned, I've got [X] years of experience in [field] and I'm confident I could contribute to [Company]'s goals. I've attached my CV for your reference – let me know if you need anything else.

Ngā mihi,
[Your Full Name]

Note: "Ngā mihi" is a Māori phrase meaning "thank you" or "greetings" – using it shows cultural awareness and respect.

Response: "Fair enough. If my skills are a strong match, would you consider sponsorship? I'm confident I can add real value to your team."

New Zealand‑Specific FAQ

Is cold calling common in New Zealand?
Yes – it's used in sales, recruitment, and increasingly in job searching. Many jobs are never advertised, making proactive outreach essential[reference:37].
Should I call or email first?
Both can work. A friendly phone call followed by a thank‑you email is often effective. Kiwis appreciate genuine interest and follow‑up[reference:38].
What is the AEWV English requirement?
From 1 June 2026, AEWV applicants for Skill Level 3 occupations need a minimum IELTS score of 4.0 or equivalent[reference:39].

The IFMOSA 5‑Step Method – Australia & New Zealand Edition

This is our proprietary framework, adapted for the ANZ market. Follow these steps to maximise your success rate.

1. Identify Target Companies

Focus on companies with a history of hiring international talent. Priority sectors:

  • Australia: Healthcare, construction, IT, mining, renewable energy, aged care.
  • New Zealand: Healthcare, construction, IT, hospitality, agriculture.

Australia tip: Follow projects, not just job ads – infrastructure, mining, and renewable energy projects often create unadvertised roles[reference:40].

New Zealand tip: Check the Immigration New Zealand accredited employer list – there are over 24,800 accredited employers in 2026[reference:41].

2. Find the Decision‑Maker

ANZ hiring managers are often Department Heads, HR Managers, or Team Leaders. LinkedIn is widely used in both countries.

Title tips:

  • Australia: "Talent Acquisition Manager", "HR Manager", "Head of [Department]"
  • New Zealand: "People and Culture Manager", "Talent Acquisition Lead", "Hiring Manager"

3. Get Their Direct Contact

Email patterns:

  • Australia: firstname.lastname@company.com.au
  • New Zealand: firstname.lastname@company.co.nz

Many ANZ professionals list their work phone on LinkedIn or the company website. If you can't find a direct number, call the main line and ask politely to be connected.

4. Make the Call – Country‑Specific Scripts

The script you use should match the country's communication style. See the country‑specific guides above for tailored scripts.

General ANZ script (start here):
"G'day / Kia ora [Hiring Manager’s Name], this is [Your Name]. I'm calling because I'm really interested in [Company Name] and the work your team is doing in [area]. I have a background in [skill] and I'd love to learn about any upcoming opportunities. Do you have a few minutes?"

If you need visa sponsorship, ask after establishing interest:

"Before I take more of your time – I'm currently based outside Australia/New Zealand and would need sponsorship. Is that something your company is able to support for the right candidate?"

5. Follow Up Professionally

Send a thank‑you email within 24 hours. Australians appreciate concise, direct follow‑ups. New Zealanders appreciate a friendly, genuine approach. Persistence is valued in both countries – but avoid being pushy.

Frequently Asked Questions – Cold Calling in Australia & New Zealand

Is cold calling effective in Australia and New Zealand?
Yes – when done professionally. Australians appreciate a direct approach, while New Zealanders respond well to a friendly, relationship‑focused style. About 10% of cold calls turn into positive conversations[reference:42].
Do I need to speak the local language?
English is the primary language. In New Zealand, using Māori greetings like "Kia ora" shows cultural awareness and is appreciated.
What is the best way to find a hiring manager's name in ANZ?
LinkedIn is widely used. In Australia, you can also call the main switchboard and ask. In New Zealand, checking the company website or calling to ask for the hiring manager's name is common[reference:43].
Can I call a hiring manager without a job offer?
Yes – many ANZ roles are never advertised[reference:44]. A proactive, well‑prepared call can uncover hidden opportunities.
How long should my call be?
Keep it under 5 minutes unless the manager engages you further. Be respectful of their time.
What if they say "apply online"?
Thank them, ask if you can mention you called in your cover letter, and then apply. The call creates a personal touch that can make your application stand out.
