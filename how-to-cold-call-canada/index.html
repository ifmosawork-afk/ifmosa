Why Cold Calling Works in Canada

Canada has a welcoming immigration system and a strong economy. However, the job market is competitive, especially for international candidates. Cold calling allows you to:

  • Bypass the ATS and speak directly to hiring managers.
  • Discover unadvertised roles – many Canadian companies hire through networking.
  • Demonstrate initiative – a quality highly valued by Canadian employers.

Visa context: The most common routes are LMIA‑based work permits and the Global Talent Stream (2‑week processing for tech roles). Many Canadian employers are familiar with these pathways.

The IFMOSA 5‑Step Method – Canada Edition

1. Identify Target Companies

Focus on companies that have a history of hiring international workers. Use the Government of Canada's employer resources to identify LMIA‑approved employers. Priority sectors: tech, healthcare, construction, and agriculture.

2. Find the Decision‑Maker

Canadian hiring managers are often Department Heads, Team Leads, or HR Managers. LinkedIn is widely used in Canada – search for these titles.

3. Get Their Direct Contact

Use email patterns like firstname.lastname@company.ca or firstname@company.com. Many Canadian professionals list their work phone on LinkedIn or company websites.

4. Make the Call – Use This Canada‑Specific Script

"Hi [Hiring Manager’s Name], this is [Your Name]. I'm calling because I'm very interested in [Company Name] and the work your team is doing in [area]. I have a background in [skill] and I'd love to learn about any upcoming opportunities. Do you have a few minutes?"

If you need LMIA or GTS sponsorship, ask after establishing interest:

"Before I take more of your time – I'm currently based outside Canada and would need sponsorship. Is that something your company is able to support for the right candidate?"

5. Follow Up Professionally

Send a thank‑you email within 24 hours. Canadian employers value polite, professional follow‑ups. If you don't get through, leave a brief voicemail and try 2–3 times over a week.

Canada‑Specific Scripts

Scenario 1: You Found an Open Role

"Hi [Name], I saw your job posting for [Role] on [Platform]. I'm very interested because my experience in [skill] matches what you're looking for. I wanted to call and introduce myself – do you have a moment?"

Scenario 2: Exploring Unadvertised Roles

"Hello [Name], I've been following [Company] and I'm impressed by [recent project]. I'm a [profession] with [X] years of experience. I was wondering if you have any upcoming needs in your team that aren't advertised yet."

Scenario 3: You Need LMIA or GTS Sponsorship

"Hi [Name], I'm reaching out because I'm very interested in [Company]. I have [X] years in [field]. I'm currently outside Canada and would need sponsorship. Before we go further – is that something your company is open to?"

Best Time to Call Canadian Hiring Managers

  • Days: Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday.
  • Time: 10:00 – 11:30 AM (EST) or 2:00 – 4:00 PM (EST).
  • Time zones: If targeting Western Canada (Vancouver), call between 10 AM – 12 PM PST (1 – 3 PM EST).

Handling Common Canada Objections

"We don't do LMIA."

Response: "I understand – is that a fixed policy, or is it role‑dependent? I'm open to exploring other pathways like the Global Talent Stream if that's possible. Thank you for being upfront."

"We prefer Canadian citizens/PRs."

Response: "I completely understand the preference. If my skills are a strong match, would you be open to considering sponsorship for the right candidate? I'm confident I can add significant value."
