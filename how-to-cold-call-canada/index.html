Why Cold Calling Works in Canada

Canada has a welcoming immigration system and a strong economy, but the job market is competitive. Cold calling allows you to:

  • Bypass the ATS and speak directly to hiring managers.
  • Discover unadvertised roles – many Canadian companies hire through networking.
  • Demonstrate initiative – a quality highly valued by Canadian employers.

Visa context – 2026:

  • LMIA (Labour Market Impact Assessment): Employers must prove no Canadian worker is available. Processing times vary from 8‑20 weeks.
  • Global Talent Stream (GTS): 2‑week processing for tech roles. Requires a job offer and salary meeting the median wage.
  • Express Entry / PNP: Points‑based systems. A job offer with LMIA or provincial nomination adds significant points.
  • Work permit exemptions: Certain roles (e.g., intra‑company transfers, performers) are LMIA‑exempt under IMP.

Canadian Hiring Culture & Regional Nuances

Canadian business culture is polite, friendly, and networking‑driven. However, approaches vary by province:

  • General: Canadians are polite but direct. Use "please" and "thank you" extensively. First names are used quickly, but titles matter in formal industries.
  • British Columbia (BC): Relaxed, environmentally conscious, tech‑driven. Hiring managers appreciate a human‑centred approach.
  • Ontario (ON): Fast‑paced, finance and tech hub. Direct but courteous communication is valued. Toronto is very multicultural.
  • Quebec (QC): French‑first. Cold calling in French is preferred, but English is accepted in Montreal tech/startups. Relationship‑building is slower and more formal.
  • Alberta (AB): Energy and construction focused. Direct, friendly, and value‑oriented. "Cowboy" casualness mixed with professionalism.

🇨🇦 British Columbia – Tech, Healthcare, Film

VancouverVictoriaKelowna

Cultural Nuances

BC is progressive, eco‑conscious, and relationship‑oriented. Hiring managers value a human‑centred approach. The tech and film industries are booming.

Visa Context

  • Tech: GTS is widely used. Companies like Microsoft, Amazon, and local startups actively sponsor.
  • Healthcare: BC has a severe shortage – many employers are open to LMIA sponsorship.

Script – Exploring unadvertised roles (Vancouver tech)

"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. I hope you're having a good week. I've been following [Company]'s work in [area] – particularly your recent project on [specific achievement]. I'm a [profession] with [X] years of experience in [skill], and I'm really interested in the kind of work your team is doing. I understand you might not have an immediate opening, but I wanted to introduce myself and ask if you'd be open to a brief conversation about your team's priorities. I'd be happy to send my CV."

Common Objection

"We prefer local candidates."
Response: "I understand the preference. If my skills are a strong match, would you consider LMIA or GTS sponsorship? I'm confident I can add value."

🇨🇦 Ontario – Finance, Tech, Manufacturing

TorontoOttawaKitchener-Waterloo

Cultural Nuances

Direct but courteous. Toronto is fast‑paced, multicultural, and competitive. Hiring managers expect concise, well‑prepared calls.

Visa Context

  • Tech: GTS is common in Waterloo and Toronto. Many large banks and fintechs sponsor.
  • Manufacturing/Logistics: LMIA sponsors are prevalent in the GTA and surrounding areas.

Script – Found a job ad (Toronto finance)

"Good morning [Name], this is [Your Name]. I'm calling about your posting for the [Role] on [Platform]. I have a strong background in [field] with [X] years of experience – I've worked on [specific project] that aligns directly with what you're looking for. I don't want to take up too much of your time, but I wanted to introduce myself and ask if you'd be open to receiving my application directly. I can send my CV over immediately."

Common Objection

"We don't sponsor visas."
Response: "Is that a fixed policy, or could it vary by role? I'm open to exploring options like the GTS."

🇨🇦 Quebec – Unique Language & Cultural Context

MontrealQuebec City

Cultural Nuances

French is the primary language. An attempt to speak French, even basic, is highly appreciated. Relationship‑building is slower and more formal. In Montreal tech hubs, English is accepted but always ask first if they prefer French.

Visa Context

  • Quebec Selection Certificate (CSQ): Unique to Quebec. Employers must often prove they couldn't find a local candidate, plus obtain CSQ approval.

Script – Exploring roles (Montreal tech – bilingual)

"Bonjour [Name], this is [Your Name]. Je parle un peu français. I'm calling because I'm very interested in [Company]. I have experience in [skill] and I'm looking to bring my expertise to Quebec. I understand the cultural and language requirements here and I'm committed to integrating. Do you have a few minutes to discuss your team's current needs?"

🇨🇦 Prairies (AB, SK, MB) – Energy, Agriculture, Construction

AlbertaSaskatchewanManitoba

Cultural Nuances

Direct, friendly, and value‑oriented. "Cowboy" casualness mixed with professionalism. Employers appreciate candidates who understand the industry's practical challenges.

Visa Context

  • Agriculture/Construction: Seasonal LMIA roles are common for skilled trades.
  • Energy: Oil and gas projects often require specialised workers, leading to sponsor applications.

Script – Project‑based approach (Alberta energy)

"G'day [Name], this is [Your Name]. I see you're involved in the [Project Name] over in [Location]. I've got a background in [skill] and I've delivered [quantifiable result] on similar projects. I'm not sure if you're looking for extra hands, but I wanted to put my name forward. I'm flexible on location and timing. Do you have a minute?"

The IFMOSA 5‑Step Method – Canada Edition

1. Identify Target Companies

Focus on LMIA‑approved or GTS‑eligible employers. Check the Government of Canada's employer resources.

2. Find the Decision‑Maker

LinkedIn is widely used. Titles like "Talent Acquisition Manager", "HR Manager", or "Department Head".

3. Get Their Direct Contact

Email patterns: firstname.lastname@company.ca or firstname@company.com.

4. Make the Call

Use province‑specific scripts above.

5. Follow Up

Send a thank‑you email within 24 hours. Canadian employers value polite, professional follow‑ups.

FAQs – Cold Calling in Canada

Is cold calling effective in Canada?
Yes – Canada has a networking‑driven culture. Proactive candidates are viewed favourably.
Should I use French in Quebec?
Yes, always attempt French first in Quebec. In Montreal tech, English may be accepted, but asking politely is best.
What if they say they don't sponsor?
Thank them and move on. Confirming early saves time.
