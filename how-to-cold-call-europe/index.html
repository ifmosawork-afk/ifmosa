Why Cold Calling Works in Europe

Europe is not one market – it's five distinct regions with different hiring cultures, communication styles, and visa pathways. What works in Germany may not work in Italy. What's acceptable in Sweden might be too direct in Spain. Cold calling allows you to:

  • Bypass bureaucratic application processes that vary by country.
  • Demonstrate initiative and confidence – qualities that are valued across Europe, but expressed differently.
  • Learn about unadvertised roles in fast‑growing sectors like tech, healthcare, and engineering.

Visa context: The EU Blue Card is the most common pathway for highly skilled non‑EU professionals. It's available in 25 of 27 EU countries, with a salary threshold of €50,700 (2026). Denmark and Ireland do not participate. EFTA countries (Norway, Switzerland, Iceland, Liechtenstein) have their own systems. Each region also has national work permit schemes – we cover them below.

🌲 Nordics – Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland

🇸🇪 Sweden 🇳🇴 Norway 🇩🇰 Denmark 🇫🇮 Finland

Cultural Characteristics

  • Flat hierarchies – managers are approachable, but decisions are often consensus‑driven.
  • Direct but humble – avoid boasting; the "Law of Jante" discourages overt self‑promotion.
  • Finland values straightforwardness and action‑orientation.
  • Sweden involves more people in the hiring process – patience is required.
  • Norway – professionals are concise and direct; meetings go straight to the point.
  • Silence is normal – especially in Finland, pauses are not awkward; they're part of the conversation.
  • Do Not Call registers – check Denmark's Robinson List and Sweden's NIX register before dialing.

Visa Context

  • Sweden, Finland – EU Blue Card available.
  • Denmark – does not participate in the EU Blue Card; has its own work permit scheme (Pay Limit Scheme, Positive List).
  • Norway – not an EU member; has its own skilled worker visa (requires job offer and salary threshold).

Best Time to Call

  • Days: Tuesday – Thursday.
  • Time: 10:00 – 11:30 AM or 2:00 – 4:00 PM local time.
  • Avoid: Monday mornings, Friday afternoons, and national holidays.

Scripts – Nordic Style (direct, humble, no fluff)

Scenario A: You found a job ad

"Hej [Name], this is [Your Name]. I saw your posting for the [Role] position. I've been working in [industry] for [X] years and I think my background in [specific skill] could be a good fit. I'm not looking for a long conversation right now – I just wanted to introduce myself and ask if you're open to a brief chat about the role. I can send my CV over afterwards if that's easier."

Scenario B: Exploring unadvertised opportunities

"Hello [Name], this is [Your Name]. I'm calling because I've been following [Company] and I'm genuinely impressed by your work in [area]. I have a background in [skill] and I was wondering if you have any upcoming needs in your team that aren't advertised yet – maybe later this year or early next year. I don't expect an answer now, just wanted to put myself on your radar."

Scenario C: You need sponsorship

"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. I'm interested in [Company] because of [specific reason]. I'm currently based outside the EU and would need sponsorship. I know that might be a hurdle, but I wanted to ask directly – is your company open to sponsoring the right candidate, or is that not something you do? I appreciate you being straight with me."

Follow‑up email (after a voicemail)

"Subject: Quick follow‑up – [Your Name] – [Role] interest

Hej [Name],

I left a voicemail earlier today – I'm interested in [Company] and wanted to introduce myself. I have [X] years of experience in [skill] and I'm keen to learn if you have any upcoming opportunities. I've attached my CV for reference. Let me know if you'd like to have a brief call – I can be flexible.

Thanks,
[Your Name]"

Common Objection

"We prefer local candidates."

Response: "I understand. If my skills are a strong match, would you consider sponsorship? I'm confident I can add value."

Nordic‑Specific FAQ

Is cold calling common in the Nordics?
It's less common than in the US, but respected when done professionally. A brief, well‑prepared call is viewed positively.
Should I speak English or the local language?
English is widely spoken in business – use English unless you are fluent in the local language.

🏛️ Western Europe – Germany, France, Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg

🇩🇪 Germany 🇫🇷 France 🇳🇱 Netherlands 🇧🇪 Belgium 🇱🇺 Luxembourg

Cultural Characteristics

  • Germany – formal, conservative, and trust‑based. Deals take longer; data privacy is crucial.
  • France – relationship‑oriented; initial formality is expected. Cold calling is common in sales roles.
  • Netherlands – direct and pragmatic. Dutch business culture values efficiency and straight talk.
  • Belgium – varies by region; French‑speaking Wallonia is more formal, Flemish Flanders is more direct.
  • Luxembourg – international and fast‑paced; English is widely used.

Visa Context

  • Germany, France, Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg – all offer the EU Blue Card.
  • Germany also offers an 18‑month Job Seeker Visa.
  • France has the "Passeport Talent" (Talent Passport) for highly skilled professionals.
  • Netherlands has the Highly Skilled Migrant visa (fast‑track processing).

Best Time to Call

  • Days: Tuesday – Thursday.
  • Time: 10:00 – 11:30 AM or 2:00 – 4:00 PM local time.
  • Avoid: Lunch (12‑2 PM in France, 12‑1 PM in Germany).

Scripts – Western Europe (formal, structured, relationship‑building)

Scenario A: You found a job ad (Germany/France style – formal)

"Guten Morgen / Bonjour [Mr./Ms. Last Name], this is [Your Full Name]. I hope I'm not interrupting your day. I came across your job advertisement for the [Role] position on [Platform] and I was very impressed by the scope of the role. I have a background in [field] with [X] years of experience, and I believe my expertise in [specific area] could be a strong match. I'm not looking to take up much of your time right now – I simply wanted to introduce myself and ask if you have a few minutes in the coming days to discuss what you're looking for in this position. I can send my application materials ahead of that call."

Scenario B: Exploring unadvertised opportunities (Netherlands style – direct and pragmatic)

"Good morning [Name], this is [Your Name]. I'm calling because I've been following [Company] and I see you're a key player in [industry]. I have a strong track record in [skill] and I'm currently exploring opportunities where I can bring that experience. I understand you might not have anything open right now, but I wanted to ask – what are the main challenges your team is facing this year, and is there any area where you could use additional support? I'm not asking for a job, I'm asking what problems you're trying to solve."

Scenario C: You need sponsorship (France – polite and diplomatic)

"Bonjour [Mr./Ms. Last Name], this is [Your Name]. I'm writing to you because I have a strong interest in [Company] and I believe my profile in [field] could be valuable to your team. I want to be fully transparent: I am currently based outside the EU and would require sponsorship. I understand this can be a constraint, and I don't want to waste your time if it's not possible. Could you kindly let me know if your company is open to sponsoring the right candidate, or if I should focus my search elsewhere? I appreciate your honesty."

Follow‑up email (formal, polite)

"Subject: Follow‑up – [Your Name] – [Role] interest

Dear Mr./Ms. [Last Name],

Thank you for taking the time to speak with me earlier today. I very much appreciated your insights into the [Role] position and the challenges your team is currently facing. As discussed, I have extensive experience in [specific area] and I am confident I could contribute meaningfully to your objectives.

I have attached my CV and a brief portfolio of my work for your reference. I would welcome the opportunity to continue the conversation at your convenience. Please let me know a time that suits you.

Yours sincerely,
[Your Full Name]"

Common Objection

"We don't sponsor EU Blue Cards."

Response: "I understand. Is that a fixed policy, or could it vary by role? I'm open to exploring other visa options."

Western European FAQ

Is cold calling acceptable in Germany and France?
In Germany, it's more accepted in sales and consulting. In France, it's acceptable if you're polite and well‑prepared.
Should I use formal titles?
Yes – use "Mr./Ms." and academic/professional titles (Dr., Prof.) in Germany and France. In the Netherlands and Luxembourg, first names are common after initial contact.

🏔️ Central Europe – Switzerland, Austria, Poland, Czech Republic, Hungary

🇨🇭 Switzerland 🇦🇹 Austria 🇵🇱 Poland 🇨🇿 Czech Republic 🇭🇺 Hungary

Cultural Characteristics

  • Switzerland – formal, precise, and reserved. Punctuality is non‑negotiable.
  • Austria – similar to Germany but slightly more relaxed; titles matter.
  • Poland – fast‑growing and performance‑driven. Cold calling is common in sales roles. Open feedback culture.
  • Czech Republic – relationship‑focused; initial formality is expected before moving to a more direct style.
  • Hungary – Budapest is a major shared‑services hub; business culture is becoming more Westernised.

Visa Context

  • Switzerland – not an EU member; has its own work permit system (L/B permits). Requires job offer and quota approval.
  • Austria, Poland, Czech Republic, Hungary – EU members; offer the EU Blue Card.
  • Poland – also has a simplified work permit system for certain nationals.

Best Time to Call

  • Days: Tuesday – Thursday.
  • Time: 10:00 – 11:30 AM or 2:00 – 4:00 PM local time.

Scripts – Central Europe (professional, performance‑focused)

Scenario A: You found a job ad (Switzerland/Austria – formal, concise)

"Good morning [Mr./Ms. Last Name], this is [Your Name]. I'm calling regarding your open position for [Role], which I found on [Platform]. I have a solid background in [field] with [X] years of experience, and I'm very interested in the opportunity. I don't want to take too much of your time now – I'm simply reaching out to introduce myself and to ask if you'd be open to receiving my application directly. I can send it over right after this call if you'd like."

Scenario B: Exploring unadvertised roles (Poland – direct, results‑oriented)

"Hello [Name], this is [Your Name]. I'm calling because I've been tracking [Company]'s growth in [sector] and I see you're expanding. I have a strong track record in [specific skill] – I've delivered [quantifiable result, e.g., 20% revenue growth, 100+ projects] in my previous roles. I'm wondering if your team has any upcoming hiring plans that might benefit from someone with that profile. I'm flexible on timing and I'm happy to have a brief chat whenever it's convenient for you."

Scenario C: You need sponsorship (Czech Republic – relationship‑focused)

"Good morning [Name], this is [Your Name]. I've been following [Company] and I admire your work in [area]. I have experience in [skill] and I'm currently looking for a new challenge. I want to be upfront – I'm not based in the EU and I would need sponsorship. I know that might be a complication, but I wanted to ask if your company has ever supported international hires in similar roles, and if that's something you'd consider for the right profile."

Follow‑up email (formal, with attached CV)

"Subject: Follow‑up – [Your Name] – [Role] interest

Dear [Name],

Thank you for taking a moment to speak with me today. As we discussed, I have [X] years of experience in [field] and I'm very interested in contributing to [Company]'s objectives in [area].

I've attached my CV and a brief summary of my key achievements for your reference. I'm available for a longer conversation at your convenience – just let me know a time that works for you.

Best regards,
[Your Name]"

Common Objection

"We don't have a sponsor licence."

Response: "I understand. Is that something the company is considering? I appreciate your honesty."

Central European FAQ

Is English enough for business calls?
In Poland, Czech Republic, and Hungary, English is widely used in multinational companies. In Switzerland and Austria, German is preferred but English is often acceptable.

☀️ Southern Europe – Italy, Spain, Portugal, Greece, Malta

🇮🇹 Italy 🇪🇸 Spain 🇵🇹 Portugal 🇬🇷 Greece 🇲🇹 Malta

Cultural Characteristics

  • Italy – relationship‑driven; personal connections matter. Formality is expected initially.
  • Spain – warm and personable; building rapport is important before getting to business.
  • Portugal – friendly and welcoming; English is widely spoken in business.
  • Greece – relationship‑oriented; patience and persistence are key.
  • Malta – international and fast‑growing; English is an official language.

Visa Context

  • Italy, Spain, Portugal, Greece, Malta – all offer the EU Blue Card.
  • Portugal – also offers a D3 tech visa and startup visas.
  • Spain – has a startup visa and digital nomad visa.

Best Time to Call

  • Days: Tuesday – Thursday.
  • Time: 10:00 – 11:30 AM or 3:00 – 5:00 PM local time.
  • Avoid: 2‑4 PM (siesta hours in Spain and Italy).

Scripts – Southern Europe (warm, personable, relationship‑building)

Scenario A: You found a job ad (Italy/Spain – warm, polite)

"Buongiorno / Buenos días [Mr./Ms. Last Name], this is [Your Name]. I hope you're having a good day. I'm calling because I saw your job posting for the [Role] position and I was genuinely excited by the opportunity. I have a background in [field] and I've worked on [specific project] which I think could be relevant to what you're building. I don't want to take up too much of your time – I just wanted to introduce myself personally and ask if you might be open to reviewing my profile. I can send my CV and a brief note about my experience right after this call."

Scenario B: Exploring unadvertised roles (Portugal – friendly and open)

"Hello [Name], this is [Your Name]. I've been following [Company] with great interest – especially your recent work in [area]. I have experience in [skill] and I'm currently exploring opportunities where I can apply that experience. I understand you might not have an immediate opening, but I was wondering if you could share a bit about your team's priorities for the coming months – maybe there's a project where an extra pair of hands could be valuable. I'd be happy to have a brief coffee chat (virtual or in person) to introduce myself."

Scenario C: You need sponsorship (Greece – patient, relationship‑oriented)

"Good morning [Name], this is [Your Name]. I hope I'm not calling at a bad time. I'm very interested in [Company] – I've been following your work in [sector] and I think my background in [field] could be a good fit. I want to be honest with you: I'm currently based outside the EU and I would need sponsorship. I understand this can be a challenge, and I don't want to waste your time if it's not possible. Could you tell me if your company has ever sponsored international hires in similar roles? I'm happy to provide any additional information you might need."

Southern European FAQ

Is cold calling common in Southern Europe?
It's less common than in Northern Europe, but can be effective if you build rapport and show genuine interest.
Should I speak the local language?
English is often used in multinational companies, but showing effort in the local language is appreciated.

The IFMOSA 5‑Step Method – Europe Edition

This is our proprietary framework, adapted for Europe's diverse markets. Follow these steps to maximise your success rate.

1. Identify Target Companies

Focus on companies with a history of hiring international talent. Use the EU's official resources to identify employer‑sponsor lists. Priority sectors: tech, engineering, healthcare, and finance.

Region tips: In Germany, check the Federal Office for Migration (BAMF) resources. In the Nordics, look for companies with English‑language career pages – they're more likely to sponsor.

2. Find the Decision‑Maker

European hiring managers are often Department Heads, HR Managers, or Team Leads. LinkedIn is widely used across Europe, but titles vary by country:

  • Germany: "Abteilungsleiter" (Department Head), "Personalleiter" (HR Manager)
  • France: "Responsable de département", "Directeur des ressources humaines"
  • Spain: "Jefe de departamento", "Director de recursos humanos"
  • Nordics: Often English titles like "Head of", "Manager" – many prefer English

3. Get Their Direct Contact

Email patterns vary by country:

  • Germany: firstname.lastname@company.de
  • France: prenom.nom@company.fr
  • Netherlands: firstname.lastname@company.nl
  • Nordics: Often firstname.lastname@company.se/.no/.dk/.fi

Company websites often list direct numbers for department heads. In the Nordics, many professionals are reachable via LinkedIn.

4. Make the Call – Region‑Specific Scripts

The script you use should match the region's communication style. See the region‑specific guides above for tailored scripts.

General Europe script (start here):
"Good morning/afternoon [Hiring Manager’s Name], this is [Your Name]. I'm calling because I'm very interested in [Company Name] and the work your team is doing in [area]. I have a background in [skill] and I'd love to learn about any upcoming opportunities. Do you have a few minutes?"

If you need EU Blue Card or work permit sponsorship, ask after establishing interest:

"Before I take more of your time – I'm currently based outside the EU and would need sponsorship. Is that something your company is able to support for the right candidate?"

5. Follow Up Professionally

Send a thank‑you email within 24 hours. In Germany and France, follow‑ups should be formal and polite. In the Nordics, a brief, direct follow‑up is appreciated. In Southern Europe, personal warmth matters. Persistence is valued across Europe, but the tone should match the local culture.

Frequently Asked Questions – Cold Calling in Europe

Is cold calling effective across all of Europe?
Yes, but the approach must be tailored to the region. A direct, concise script works in the Nordics, while Southern Europe requires more relationship‑building.
Do I need to speak the local language?
English is widely accepted in business, especially in tech and multinational companies. However, showing effort in the local language is always appreciated.
What is the EU Blue Card salary threshold for 2026?
The general threshold is €50,700 gross per year. For shortage occupations, it's €45,934.20. These vary by country – check the specific national requirements.
Can I call a hiring manager without a job offer?
Yes – many European companies are open to speculative applications. Focus on your skills and what you can bring to the team.
How long should my call be?
Keep it under 5 minutes unless the manager engages you further. Be respectful of their time.
What if they say "apply online"?
Thank them, ask if you can mention you called in your cover letter, and then apply. The call creates a personal touch that can make your application stand out.
