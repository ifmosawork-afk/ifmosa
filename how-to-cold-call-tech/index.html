Why Cold Calling Works in Tech

Tech hiring is fast‑paced, meritocratic, and often bypasses traditional HR gatekeepers. Cold calling allows you to:

  • Reach engineering managers directly (they often skip formal recruiting).
  • Demonstrate technical communication skills and confidence.
  • Discover unadvertised roles – many startups hire based on referrals and direct outreach.

Global Visa Context – 2026:

  • USA (H‑1B): Lottery system. $60,000+ salary. Tech companies are the biggest filers.
  • UK (Tech Nation / Skilled Worker): Global Talent visa for tech leaders. Skilled Worker for engineers. Sponsor list available.
  • Canada (GTS): 2‑week processing for tech roles. LMIA‑exempt in many cases.
  • EU (Blue Card): €50,700 threshold (reduced for IT). Valid in 25 countries.
  • Australia (TSS 482): AUD $73,150 threshold. Many IT roles on the skilled list.

Tech Hiring Culture

  • Fast‑paced: Tech managers are busy. Calls must be concise (under 5 minutes).
  • Meritocratic: Past projects and GitHub matter more than CVs. Reference your portfolio.
  • Recruitment‑savvy: Many engineering managers have been recruited themselves – they respect direct approaches.
  • Data‑driven: Use numbers to describe your impact (e.g., "Reduced latency by 30%").

Tech Visa Sponsorship – What to Say

Tech managers are often aware of visa pathways. Approach sponsorship as a "cost of acquiring top talent".

Script – Addressing Sponsorship in Tech

"Before I take more of your time – I want to be upfront that I'm currently based in [Country] and would need sponsorship. I know tech companies like yours are often familiar with [H‑1B/GTS/EU Blue Card]. Is that something your team is able to support for the right engineer?"

Tech‑Specific Scripts

Scenario A: You found a job ad (Software Engineer)

"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. I saw your posting for the [Role] and I'm very interested. I've got [X] years of experience with [Tech Stack]. I actually worked on [Project] that's similar to what you're building. I've got my GitHub and portfolio ready if you'd like to take a look. Do you have a few minutes to chat?"

Scenario B: Exploring unadvertised roles (Data Scientist)

"Hello [Name], this is [Your Name]. I've been following [Company]'s work in [ML/AI/Data] – particularly your recent paper on [Topic]. I've got a background in [Skill] and I'd love to learn about any upcoming roles in your data team. I'm not just looking for any job – I'm interested in the specific problems you're solving."

Scenario C: Following up after a cold email

"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] – I sent you a note on LinkedIn yesterday about the [Role]. I know things get busy, so I wanted to call and see if you had a chance to glance at my profile. I'm really keen on the work your team is doing in [Area]."

The IFMOSA 5‑Step Method – Tech Edition

1. Identify Target Companies

Use the official sponsor lists (USCIS, UK Home Office, Canada GTS list). Look for companies with high visa filing numbers.

2. Find the Decision‑Maker

Tech managers often have titles like "Engineering Manager", "Director of Engineering", "CTO".

3. Get Their Direct Contact

firstname.lastname@company.com is the standard. Many tech pros are on GitHub and LinkedIn.

4. Make the Call

Be concise. 2‑3 minutes max. Always offer to send a portfolio or GitHub.

5. Follow Up

Send a follow‑up email with links to your GitHub, LinkedIn, and portfolio. Tech managers appreciate visual proof of skill.

FAQs – Cold Calling in Tech

Is cold calling effective in tech?
Yes – tech managers often hire based on direct outreach. A concise, prepared call can bypass the ATS entirely.
What if I don't have a degree?
In tech, projects and experience matter more than degrees. Reference your portfolio and GitHub.
Should I mention my visa status immediately?
Lead with your skills. If they engage, ask about sponsorship after establishing interest.
