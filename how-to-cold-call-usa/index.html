Why Cold Calling Works in the USA

The US job market is fast‑paced and highly competitive. Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) filter out up to 75% of online applications before a human ever sees them. Cold calling bypasses this entirely – you go straight to the decision‑maker.

Key advantages:

  • Demonstrates confidence and initiative – qualities US employers highly value.
  • Reveals unadvertised roles – many US companies fill positions through networking and referrals.
  • Accelerates the hiring process – you can get a verbal offer or interview scheduled on the spot.

Visa context: The most common route is the H‑1B visa (specialty occupations). Many US employers are familiar with the process, but you need to approach the conversation correctly – lead with your skills, not your visa status.

The IFMOSA 5‑Step Method – USA Edition

1. Identify Target Companies

Focus on companies that have a documented history of H‑1B filings. Use the US Department of Labor’s H‑1B employer database or tools like MyVisaJobs.com. Target industries: tech, healthcare, engineering, and finance.

2. Find the Decision‑Maker

In US companies, the hiring manager is often a Department Head, Team Lead, or HR Manager. Use LinkedIn and the company website to find names. If you can’t, call the main line and ask for the person responsible for hiring in your department.

3. Get Their Direct Contact

US business culture is open – many professionals list their work phone or email on LinkedIn. Alternatively, use email pattern: firstname.lastname@company.com. If you have a name, you can often call the company switchboard and ask to be transferred directly.

4. Make the Call – Use This US‑Specific Script

"Hi [Hiring Manager’s Name], this is [Your Name]. I’m calling because I’m really interested in [Company Name] – specifically the work your team is doing in [area]. I have a background in [skill] and I’d love to learn if you have any upcoming openings. Do you have a couple of minutes?"

If you need H‑1B sponsorship, mention it only after establishing interest:

"Before I take more of your time – just to confirm, is your company able to support H‑1B sponsorship for the right candidate?"

5. Follow Up Like a Pro

Send a thank‑you email within 24 hours. Reiterate your interest and summarise the key points from the call. If you didn’t get through, leave a voicemail and try again 2–3 times over a week. US employers respect persistence – it shows you’re serious.

USA‑Specific Scripts

Scenario 1: You Found an Open Role

"Hi [Name], I saw your job posting for [Role] on LinkedIn. I’m very interested because my background in [skill] matches what you’re looking for. I wanted to call and introduce myself – do you have a moment?"

Scenario 2: Exploring Unadvertised Roles

"Hello [Name], I’ve been following [Company] for a while and I’m impressed by [recent project]. I’m a [profession] with [X] years of experience. I was wondering if you have any future needs in your team that might not be advertised yet."

Scenario 3: You Need H‑1B Sponsorship

"Hi [Name], I’m reaching out because I’m very interested in [Company]. I have [X] years in [field]. I’m currently based outside the US and would need H‑1B sponsorship. Before we go further, I wanted to ask – is that something your company is open to?"

Best Time to Call US Hiring Managers

  • Days: Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday.
  • Time: 10:00 – 11:30 AM (EST) or 2:00 – 4:00 PM (EST). Avoid Monday mornings and Friday afternoons.
  • Time zones: If targeting the West Coast, adjust for PST – call between 10 AM – 12 PM PST (1 – 3 PM EST).

Handling Common USA Objections

"We don't sponsor H‑1B."

Response: "I understand – is that a fixed policy, or is it role‑dependent? I’m open to exploring other visa options if that’s possible. Thank you for being upfront."

"Just apply online."

Response: "I absolutely will, and I wanted to call first to understand more about the role so I can tailor my application. Could you tell me what you’re most looking for in this position?"

Absolutely – but lead with your skills and experience first. Ask about sponsorship only once you’ve established interest.
