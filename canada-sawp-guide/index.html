Seasonal Agricultural Worker Program – Work in Canadian Farms

The Seasonal Agricultural Worker Program (SAWP) is a government‑to‑government program that has been operating since 1966 for Caribbean nations and 1974 for Mexico. It allows workers from participating countries to fill seasonal labour shortages in Canadian agriculture, primarily in crop production, horticulture, and harvesting. This guide covers eligibility, participating countries, wages (AEWR), housing, transportation, and the pathway to permanent residence.

📌 What is the Seasonal Agricultural Worker Program (SAWP)?

SAWP is a structured government‑to‑government program. Workers are placed through a managed recruitment system involving the source country government and its worker recruitment infrastructure. Mexican SAWP workers are placed through the Secretaría del Trabajo y Previsión Social. Caribbean workers are placed through liaison services in each participating country. Key features:

  • Participating Countries (2026): Mexico, Antigua and Barbuda, Barbados, Dominica, Grenada, Jamaica, Montserrat, St. Kitts‑Nevis, St. Lucia, St. Vincent and the Grenadines, and Trinidad and Tobago.
  • Maximum stay: Up to 8 months per calendar year. Workers must return home at the end of each season.
  • Work type: Seasonal primary agricultural work – crop production, horticulture, harvesting. Not available for food processing, machinery maintenance, or farm management.
  • Pathway to PR: SAWP itself does not lead directly to permanent residence, but workers can transition to the Agri‑Food Pilot after accumulating 12 months of Canadian agricultural work experience (over multiple seasons).

Official source: IRCC – Agricultural Workers.

💰 SAWP Wages – Adverse Effect Wage Rate (AEWR) by Province (2026)

Employers must pay at least the provincial Adverse Effect Wage Rate (AEWR) for the specific commodity. The AEWR is updated annually by Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC). Examples:

Piece rates may also be used if they provide earnings at least equal to the AEWR. Many workers earn above the minimum wage due to overtime and productivity bonuses.

🏠 Housing & Transportation – Key Worker Protections

  • Free housing: Employers must provide suitable housing that meets provincial health and safety standards. No rent deduction is allowed.
  • Transportation to Canada: Employers must reimburse inbound travel costs after the worker completes 50% of the contract period.
  • Daily transportation: Employers must provide or reimburse daily transportation to and from the worksite if not located on the housing premises.
  • Three‑quarters guarantee: Employers must guarantee employment for at least 75% of the workdays in the contract period, regardless of weather or crop conditions.
  • Health insurance: Employers are required to provide workers’ compensation coverage and health insurance.

📝 How to Apply for SAWP – Step‑by‑Step

  1. Register with your home country’s recruitment office – In Mexico: STPS (Secretaría del Trabajo y Previsión Social); in Caribbean nations: local liaison services.
  2. Canadian employer requests workers through the government‑managed system.
  3. Employer obtains LMIA – SAWP falls under the Primary Agriculture stream of the TFWP. LMIA processing: 2‑4 weeks.
  4. Receive job placement through the government system – you do not apply directly to employers.
  5. Apply for work permit at the Canadian embassy/consulate (with employer’s LMIA and job offer). No English test required.
  6. Travel to Canada and begin work – Housing is provided by the employer.

Total timeline: 2‑4 months from registration to arrival in Canada.

🟢 SAWP to Permanent Residence – Agri‑Food Pilot (AFP)

While SAWP itself does not directly lead to permanent residence, workers can transition to the Agri‑Food Pilot (AFP) after accumulating 12 months of Canadian agricultural work experience (cumulative over multiple SAWP seasons). The AFP offers a direct permanent residence pathway for agricultural workers.

  • Requirement: 12 months (1,560 hours) of Canadian agricultural work experience in eligible industries (meat processing, greenhouse, livestock, etc.).
  • Language: CLB 4 minimum (IELTS 4.0).
  • Education: High school diploma (or ECA for foreign education).
  • Job offer: Must have a job offer from a Canadian employer in an eligible agricultural sector.
  • Processing: 6‑12 months for permanent residence.

Many SAWP workers transition to permanent residence through the Agri‑Food Pilot after 12 months of cumulative work experience. Official source: IRCC – Agri‑Food Pilot.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions – Canada SAWP

Which countries participate in SAWP?
Mexico plus 10 Caribbean nations (Jamaica, Trinidad and Tobago, Barbados, Dominica, Grenada, St. Lucia, St. Vincent and the Grenadines, St. Kitts‑Nevis, Montserrat, Antigua and Barbuda).
How long can I stay in Canada under SAWP?
Up to 8 months per calendar year. You must return home at the end of each season.
Is housing provided?
Yes. Employers are required to provide suitable housing at no direct cost to workers.
Can SAWP lead to permanent residence?
Not directly, but you can transition to the Agri‑Food Pilot after 12 months of cumulative agricultural work experience (over multiple seasons).
What is the minimum wage for SAWP workers?
Varies by province. For Ontario (most common SAWP destination), the minimum AEWR is $17.60/hour for 2026 SAWP LMIAs.
Do I need English for SAWP?
No formal English test is required. Basic English is helpful but not mandatory.

⚠️ Common SAWP Mistakes & Red Flags

  • Employer not approved for SAWP: Only employers approved by ESDC can hire SAWP workers. Always verify through the home country liaison office.
  • Asking workers to pay visa fees: The employer is responsible for all LMIA and work permit fees. Never pay an employer for visa processing.
  • No three‑quarters guarantee in contract: The contract must explicitly state the 75% work guarantee.
  • Housing deductions: SAWP employers cannot deduct rent from wages – housing must be free.
  • Unofficial recruiters: Apply only through your home country’s government‑managed system. Avoid private agents promising “guaranteed” SAWP spots.
