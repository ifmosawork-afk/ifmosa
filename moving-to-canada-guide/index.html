Moving to Canada Guide 2026

Your Complete Step‑by‑Step Newcomer Settlement Roadmap

Moving to Canada is a life‑changing decision. This comprehensive guide walks you through everything you need to know: from the moment you land to settling into your new community. We cover cost of living, housing, banking, healthcare, Social Insurance Number (SIN), settlement services, and a month‑by‑month first‑year roadmap.

📋 Your First Days in Canada – Priority Checklist

The moment you arrive, focus on these essential tasks to get settled quickly and avoid unnecessary stress.

  • ✅ Exchange currency – Get Canadian dollars for immediate expenses.
  • ✅ Apply for your Social Insurance Number (SIN) – You need this to work, file taxes, and access government benefits. Apply in person at any Service Canada centre (bring your passport and immigration documents). Application is free.[reference:0]
  • ✅ Open a Canadian bank account – Most major banks offer newcomer packages with no monthly fees for the first 6–24 months. Bring your passport, work permit/PR card, and proof of address.[reference:1]
  • ✅ Register for provincial health insurance – Apply for your provincial health card immediately. Waiting periods vary by province (see below).[reference:2]
  • ✅ Find temporary accommodation – Book an Airbnb or short‑term rental for the first 2–4 weeks while you search for a permanent place.[reference:3]
  • ✅ Get a local SIM card or mobile plan – Compare plans from Rogers, Bell, Telus, or discount carriers like Freedom Mobile and Public Mobile.
  • ✅ Register children for school – Contact your local school board for registration requirements (proof of age, residency, immunization records).[reference:4]

🏠 Housing in Canada – Renting Guide for Newcomers

Finding a place to live is one of the biggest challenges when arriving in Canada. Here's what you need to know.

  • No Canadian credit history? Landlords often accept: a larger security deposit, reference letters from past landlords or employers, proof of employment or sufficient savings, or working with newcomer‑friendly landlords.[reference:8]
  • Start with short‑term accommodation – Book an Airbnb or furnished rental for the first month. This gives you time to explore neighbourhoods and view apartments in person before signing a lease.[reference:9]
  • Watch for rental scams – Never send money before seeing a unit in person. If a deal looks too good to be true, it probably is.[reference:10]
  • Know your tenant rights – Landlords must give 24‑hour notice before entry. Rent increases are regulated by provincial caps. Eviction requires a formal legal process.[reference:11]
  • Provincial 2026 rental price guides: Toronto/Vancouver: $2,000–$2,800+; Calgary/Edmonton: $1,400–$1,900; Montreal: $1,200–$1,800; smaller cities: under $1,400.[reference:12]

🏦 Banking in Canada – Best Accounts for Newcomers

Opening a bank account should be one of your first actions. Most major banks offer newcomer packages with no monthly fees for the first 12–24 months.

  • Scotiabank – Best overall for newcomers; extensive branch network, strong digital banking, and international money transfer support.[reference:13]
  • RBC and TD – Best for customer service; both offer newcomer programs with credit cards available without Canadian credit history.[reference:14]
  • National Bank – No monthly fees for up to 3 years; ideal if you want to minimize costs early.[reference:15]
  • CIBC – Strong for investing; CIBC Smart Account for newcomers offers no monthly fee for 2 years.[reference:16]
  • Simplii Financial – No‑fee online banking with access to CIBC ATMs; welcome cash bonus for eligible new clients.[reference:17]

💡 Start building credit immediately: Get a secured credit card from your bank, use it for small purchases, and pay the full balance monthly. Your home‑country credit score does not transfer to Canada.[reference:18]

🆔 Social Insurance Number (SIN) – Your Gateway to Work

A Social Insurance Number (SIN) is a nine‑digit number required to work in Canada, pay taxes, and access government benefits.

  • Who can apply? Canadian citizens, permanent residents, and temporary residents aged 12+ who are authorized to work.[reference:30]
  • How to apply: Apply in person at any Service Canada Centre (fastest), online, or by mail. The application is free.[reference:31]
  • Documents needed: Primary document (passport, PR card, or work permit) + secondary ID (driver's licence, provincial ID, etc.).[reference:32]
  • Important: You must provide your SIN to your employer within three days of starting work.[reference:33]

🤝 Free Settlement Services for Newcomers

Canada offers free government‑funded settlement services to help newcomers integrate. These services are available to permanent residents, convention refugees, and some temporary residents.[reference:34]

  • Language training: Free Language Instruction for Newcomers to Canada (LINC) classes for English or French – beginner to advanced levels.[reference:35]
  • Employment support: Resume and cover letter workshops, job search assistance, interview preparation, and mentorship programs.[reference:36]
  • Housing and community orientation: Help finding housing, registering for schools, understanding tenant rights, and connecting with local community resources.[reference:37]
  • How to find services: Search online for "settlement services near me" or "LINC classes" in your city. Major organizations include YMCA Newcomer Centres, Catholic Crosscultural Services, and local immigrant‑serving agencies.[reference:38]

💡 IRCC update: As of September 2026, economic‑class immigrants can access settlement services for six years from the date they become permanent residents – up from previous limits.[reference:39]

❓ Frequently Asked Questions – Moving to Canada

How much money do I need to settle in Canada for the first year?
A single person should budget $25,000–$35,000 CAD for the first year (including rent, food, transportation, insurance, and initial expenses). A family of four should budget $50,000–$70,000 CAD. Settlement funds required by IRCC for permanent residence applications range from $13,000 (single) to $34,000+ (family of four).[reference:43]
Can I use my foreign driver's licence in Canada?
Yes, temporarily. In most provinces, you can use a valid foreign driver's licence for 60–90 days after arrival. After that, you must apply for a provincial driver's licence. Some countries have exchange agreements (e.g., USA, UK, Germany). Check your province's regulations before driving.[reference:44]
Do I need private health insurance during the waiting period?
Strongly recommended. If your province has a 3‑month waiting period, you are uninsured during that time. An emergency room visit can cost $3,000–$5,000. Private plans cost $150–$400 per month and can save you thousands.[reference:45]
How do I find a job as a newcomer?
Start with IFMOSA Work's verified job listings for LMIA‑sponsored roles. Also use settlement service employment support (free resume help, job search workshops), attend job fairs, and build a Canadian‑style resume (2‑3 pages, accomplishment‑focused). Consider volunteering to gain Canadian experience and references.
Can I bring my family with me?
Yes. If you have a work permit or permanent residence, you can include your spouse and dependent children in your application. Spouses may be eligible for open work permits, and children can attend Canadian public schools for free.
What is the Canada Child Benefit (CCB)?
A tax‑free monthly payment to eligible families to help with the cost of raising children under 18. Apply as soon as you arrive – payments can be substantial (up to $7,000+ per year per child depending on income).[reference:46]
