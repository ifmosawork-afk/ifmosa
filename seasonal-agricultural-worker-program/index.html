Seasonal Agricultural Worker Program (SAWP) Canada 2026

Work in Canadian Agriculture – Mexico, Caribbean & Beyond

The Seasonal Agricultural Worker Program (SAWP) is a government‑to‑government program that has been operating since 1966 for Caribbean nations and 1974 for Mexico. It allows workers from participating countries to fill seasonal labour shortages in Canadian agriculture, primarily in crop production, horticulture, and harvesting.[reference:71]

📌 What is the Seasonal Agricultural Worker Program (SAWP)?

SAWP is a structured government‑to‑government program. Workers are placed through a managed recruitment system involving the source country government and its worker recruitment infrastructure. Mexican SAWP workers are placed through the Secretaría del Trabajo y Previsión Social. Caribbean workers are placed through liaison services in each participating country.[reference:72]

  • Participating Countries (2026): Mexico, Antigua and Barbuda, Barbados, Dominica, Grenada, Jamaica, Montserrat, St. Kitts‑Nevis, St. Lucia, St. Vincent and the Grenadines, Trinidad and Tobago.[reference:73]
  • Maximum stay: Up to 8 months per calendar year. Workers must return home at the end of each season.[reference:74]
  • Work type: Seasonal primary agricultural work – crop production, horticulture, harvesting. Not available for food processing, machinery maintenance, or farm management.[reference:75]
  • Pathway to PR: SAWP itself does not lead directly to permanent residence, but workers can transition to the Agri‑Food Pilot after accumulating 12 months of Canadian agricultural work experience (over multiple seasons).[reference:76]

🏠 Employer‑Provided Housing – A Key Benefit

  • SAWP employers are required to provide suitable housing for workers at no direct cost (or minimal deduction).
  • Housing must meet provincial health and safety standards.
  • Many returning SAWP workers return to the same Canadian farm year after year, building long‑term working relationships.[reference:80]
  • Housing often includes shared kitchens, bathrooms, and common areas. Some deductions from wages may apply (typically $10‑$20/day).

🟢 SAWP to Permanent Residence – Agri‑Food Pilot

While SAWP itself does not directly lead to permanent residence, workers can transition to the Agri‑Food Pilot (AFP) after accumulating 12 months of Canadian agricultural work experience (cumulative over multiple SAWP seasons). The AFP offers a direct permanent residence pathway for agricultural workers.[reference:81]

  • Requirement: 12 months (1,560 hours) of Canadian agricultural work experience in eligible industries (meat processing, greenhouse, livestock, etc.).[reference:82]
  • Language: CLB 4 minimum (IELTS 4.0).[reference:83]
  • Education: High school diploma (or ECA for foreign education).[reference:84]
  • Job offer: Must have a job offer from a Canadian employer in an eligible agricultural sector.[reference:85]

Many SAWP workers transition to permanent residence through the Agri‑Food Pilot after 12 months of cumulative work experience.[reference:86]

📝 How to Apply for SAWP – Step‑by‑Step

  1. Register with your home country’s recruitment office – In Mexico: STPS; in Caribbean nations: local liaison services.
  2. Canadian employer requests workers through the government‑managed system.
  3. Employer obtains LMIA – SAWP falls under the Primary Agriculture stream of the TFWP.[reference:87]
  4. Receive job placement through the government system – you do not apply directly to employers.
  5. Apply for work permit at the Canadian embassy/consulate (with employer’s LMIA and job offer).
  6. Travel to Canada and begin work – Housing is provided by the employer.

⏱️ Processing Time & Family Reunification

Processing time: LMIA: 2‑4 weeks; work permit: 2‑6 weeks. Total timeline: 1‑3 months.

Family reunification: SAWP is a seasonal temporary program – family members generally do not accompany workers. However, Agri‑Food Pilot applicants (once eligible for PR) can include family members.

❓ SAWP – Frequently Asked Questions

Which countries participate in SAWP?
Mexico, Antigua and Barbuda, Barbados, Dominica, Grenada, Jamaica, Montserrat, St. Kitts‑Nevis, St. Lucia, St. Vincent and the Grenadines, and Trinidad and Tobago.[reference:88]
How long can I stay in Canada under SAWP?
Up to 8 months per calendar year. You must return home at the end of each season.[reference:89]
Is housing provided?
Yes. Employers are required to provide suitable housing (often deducted from wages at $10‑$20/day).[reference:90]
Can SAWP lead to permanent residence?
Not directly, but you can transition to the Agri‑Food Pilot after 12 months of cumulative agricultural work experience (over multiple seasons).[reference:91]
What is the minimum wage for SAWP workers?
Varies by province and commodity. For Ontario (most common SAWP destination), the minimum is $17.60/hour for 2026 SAWP LMIAs.[reference:92]
